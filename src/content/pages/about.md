I'm a data scientist and public health researcher interested in how personal and patient-generated data can help answer meaningful questions about behavior, health, and disease.

I'm currently the Senior Director of Research Science at [Evidation](https://evidation.com). I've spent the last eight years there designing and running large-scale decentralized digital research studies. I've built teams, developed and evolved research processes, and shared our findings in publications and at industry and academic conferences. I have deep expertise in using commercial wearable devices in observational research across many therapeutic areas.

Before Evidation I led research and development at Fitabase, ran programs at Quantified Self Labs, and earned my Ph.D. in public health from the SDSU/UC San Diego Joint Doctoral Program, where my dissertation looked at how people use physical activity trackers.

> To learn more about me, check out what I'm up to [now](/now/).

## Digital health

I've worked in digital health since 2008, when I authored one of the first research abstracts exploring the validity of Fitbit wearables. I firmly believe in the power of personal data, whether it's captured by a smartwatch or through app-based symptom reporting. Most of health happens outside the clinic, and digital health can help capture those experiences.

## Research methods

I translate ambiguous research objectives into rigorous, scalable research protocols and processes. That often means working across functions to right-size an approach so research stays accessible and participant-focused while keeping its scientific rigor.

## Data communication

I work across the research process, from designing participant-friendly data collection to building analysis workflows that bring the results that matter to the surface. I enjoy turning research artifacts into outward-facing work that tells a meaningful story.
